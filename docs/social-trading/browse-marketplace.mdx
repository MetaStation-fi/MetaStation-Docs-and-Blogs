# Browse the Marketplace

The marketplace lists every **active** provider on MetaStation. Two kinds share the same grid, and
telling them apart is the first thing to do.

---

## The two provider types

Three tabs sit above the grid:

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **All Providers** | Both types together |
| **Signal Providers** | Traders publishing signals from an account they own, charging a subscription fee or nothing |
| **Fund Managers** | Managers trading a dedicated account the platform provisioned, paid a share of profits |

The quickest tell on a card is the fee stat: a signal provider shows **Fee**, a fund manager shows
**Profit Share** as a percentage. The action button differs too — **Subscribe** on a signal
provider, **Allocate** on a fund manager.

→ [Fund Managers](/docs/social-trading/fund-managers) explains the second type in full.

---

## Narrowing the list

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Search** | Matches the provider's display name |
| **Market** | All Markets, **ByBit**, or **MetaStation Web3** — the venue the provider trades |
| **Provider type tabs** | All Providers, Signal Providers, Fund Managers |

That is the full set. There is no filter for asset focus, subscription price, or follower count, and
no ROI or win-rate range filter — those numbers are shown on each card to compare by eye, not to
filter on.

**The Leaderboard is the other way in.** It ranks providers by performance and is worth opening
before the grid, because it applies a minimum trade count that the raw grid does not.

---

## Reading a provider card

Each card carries:

- **Market badge** — the venue the provider trades
- **Subscribers** — how many followers are currently copying
- **Fee** or **Profit Share** — the commercial terms, depending on type
- **ROI** over 7, 30 and 90 days

A card can also read **Full**, meaning the provider has hit their maximum-followers setting and is
not accepting new subscriptions, or **Subscribed**, if you already follow them.

---

## Reading the profile past the return

Open a provider and the headline percentage is the least useful number on the page. Four others
predict your experience better:

| Metric | Why it matters more |
|---|---|
| **Maximum drawdown** | The worst peak-to-trough fall — the stretch you would have had to sit through. A 200% return with a 60% drawdown is unfollowable for most people |
| **Total trades** | Sample size. Twelve trades is a story; three hundred is a distribution |
| **Track record length** | Three profitable months cannot separate skill from a rising market |
| **Win rate, read with average win and loss** | A 40% win rate with large winners beats a 90% win rate with one catastrophic loser |

**Strategy description** — written by the provider, and the only part of the profile that is not
computed. Look for a specific claim about what they trade, on what timeframe, and how they exit.

**Rank** is the provider's position among all active providers sorted by all-time ROI. It is
recalculated on a schedule rather than live.

:::warning[Signs worth walking away from]
- A return built on a handful of trades
- Triple-digit monthly ROI with a drawdown near zero — the combination is the tell, not either number alone
- No strategy description, or one that describes a feeling rather than a method
- A sharp climb followed by silence, which usually means the strategy stopped working and the
  provider stopped trading rather than closing the profile
:::

**What good tends to look like:** a record spanning several months rather than weeks, a win rate in
a plausible band, a maximum drawdown you could actually sit through, and a description specific
enough to be wrong.

---

## Before you subscribe

Published performance is the **provider's** result on **their** account. Yours will differ
structurally — later entries, your own sizing, and the fact that you join mid-strategy.
