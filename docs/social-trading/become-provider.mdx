# Become a Provider

Providers are listed in the marketplace and earn from followers who copy their trades. Your trades
execute normally on your own account; followers' accounts replicate them, sized by rules the
follower sets.

---

## First decide which kind of provider you are

The signup flow asks this before anything else, because the two branches diverge immediately.

| | **Signal Provider** | **Fund Manager** |
|---|---|---|
| **You trade** | An account you already have and connect | A **dedicated ByBit sub-account the platform provisions for you** |
| **You are paid by** | A subscription fee you set, or nothing | A **profit share**, 10%–30% |
| **Marketplace badge** | — | **Profit-Sharing** |
| **Best when** | You already have a track record on an account you control | You want the record to start clean, on an account created for the purpose |

→ [Fund Managers](/docs/social-trading/fund-managers) covers the second branch in detail.

The choice is made at registration and shapes what you are issued, so it is worth being deliberate
about rather than clicking past.

---

## What you need before you start

- A MetaStation account in good standing
- A **master account** to nominate — the account your trades will be read from
- A strategy you can describe in a paragraph, because the description is what followers actually
  read

Your provider profile trades on either a **ByBit** account or a **MetaStation Account**. That
choice is made at registration alongside the provider type.

---

## Registering

1. Go to **Social Trade → Become a Provider**.
2. **Choose your provider type** — Signal Provider or Fund Manager.
3. Fill in the profile: display name, avatar, description, strategy name, trading style, timeframe
   and risk level, plus any social links you want shown.
4. Configure the terms — the section below covers what each setting does.
5. Accept the provider terms and submit.

:::note[Your profile goes live immediately]
There is no review queue and no waiting period. On submission your provider profile is created
**active** and appears in the marketplace straight away. Get the description and the terms right
before you submit rather than planning to fix them during a review that is not going to happen.
:::

### What each type is issued at signup

**Signal providers** are issued a **webhook URL and secret**, which is how your trades reach the
platform. You can also drive signals from a bound Telegram channel or the API.

→ [Webhook Trading](/docs/automation/webhook-trading)

**Fund managers** are additionally provisioned a **dedicated ByBit sub-account**, with API keys
scoped to it, IP-restricted to MetaStation's infrastructure, and stored encrypted. You do not
create or connect this account — it is created for you as part of registration.

## The settings that govern who follows you

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum followers** | 100 | Stops new subscriptions once reached |
| **Accepting followers** | On | Closes your book without delisting your profile |
| **Minimum copy amount** | 100 | The smallest allocation you want a follower to bring |
| **Fee type** | Free | `free`, a `fixed` fee, or a `percentage` |
| **Subscription fee** | 0 | The amount, when fee type is not free |
| **Leverage band** | 10× – 20× | The range you are telling followers you trade in |

**The leverage band is descriptive, not enforced.** It tells a follower how leveraged your strategy
runs. It does not clamp the leverage on their copied position — that is set in their own copy
settings.

**A fee of zero is a legitimate starting position.** A track record on a free profile is worth more
than a subscription nobody buys, and the fee is editable later.

---

## Writing a description followers can act on

The description is the only part of your profile you control that is not a number. Say what you
trade, on what timeframe, and how you size and exit.

Worth writing:

> "I trade BTC and ETH perpetuals on the 4-hour timeframe using support and resistance with volume
> confirmation. I risk 1–2% per trade with a fixed stop loss, and average 15–25 trades a month."

Not worth writing:

> "I trade crypto and make consistent profits."

The first tells a follower whether the strategy fits their account and their tolerance. The second
tells them nothing and reads like every profile that has ever been abandoned after a bad month.

---

## How you are ranked

Your **rank** is your position among all active providers when they are sorted by **all-time ROI**.
It is recalculated on a schedule, not live, so a good day shows up in the ranking later than it
shows up in your metrics.

The marketplace's leaderboards additionally require a **minimum of 10 trades** before a provider is
eligible, so a new profile does not appear on them on the strength of a single winning trade.

Everything else on your profile — return, win rate, maximum drawdown, trade count, follower count —
is computed from your actual trading. None of it is editable.

---

## Your responsibilities

:::warning[You are not a registered financial adviser]
Publishing a strategy is not advice, and your profile should say so. Past performance does not
predict future results, and followers can and will lose money copying you.

Inflated screenshots, guaranteed-return claims, and coordinated pump-and-dump activity are grounds
for suspension. A provider's status can be set to inactive or suspended, which removes the profile
from the marketplace.
:::

A subscription is a recurring cost to your followers whether the month goes well or badly. That is
the thing to keep in mind when deciding what to charge and when to close your book.
