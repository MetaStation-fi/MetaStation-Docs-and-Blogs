# Fund Managers

MetaStation has **two kinds of provider**, and the marketplace lists them side by side. Most of the
documentation describes signal providers, because that is the older and more common shape. This
page describes the other one.

| | Signal Provider | Fund Manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Trades on** | An account they already had | A **dedicated account the platform provisions** at signup |
| **Signals reach you via** | Webhook, Telegram or API | The same execution path |
| **Charges** | A subscription fee, or nothing | A **share of profits**, declared as a percentage |
| **Marketplace badge** | — | **Profit-Sharing** |

Everything downstream of the signal is **identical**. Your copy mode, your leverage, your margin
mode and your position sizing behave exactly as they do for a signal provider — the difference is
who is on the other side and how they are paid, not how the trade reaches you.

→ [Copy Settings](/docs/social-trading/copy-settings) covers the follower side in full, and applies
unchanged to both provider types.

---

## The account a fund manager trades

This is the part with no equivalent on the signal-provider side.

When someone registers as a fund manager, MetaStation provisions a **dedicated ByBit sub-account**
for them and issues API keys scoped to it. The manager does not connect an account they already
own; they are given one.

The keys the platform issues are:

- **Scoped to that sub-account only** — they carry no authority over any other account
- **IP-restricted**, so they are unusable from anywhere but MetaStation's own infrastructure
- **Stored encrypted**, like every other exchange credential on the platform

The permissions cover contract and spot trading, options, transfers within the account structure,
and exchange history.

:::note[Why it is provisioned rather than connected]
A signal provider's own account is theirs, and its history predates the platform. A fund manager's
account is created by MetaStation at signup, so the track record that appears in the marketplace
begins on day one and belongs entirely to trading you can see.
:::

:::tip[The managed account is kept out of your normal account list]
A Fund Manager Account is **filtered out of the standard account picker** so it cannot be confused
with your own trading slots, and does not appear alongside them by default. It is reached through
the fund-manager view rather than the ordinary slot list. If you have just registered and cannot
find the account you were issued, this is why — it is not missing.
:::

## The terms a fund manager publishes

Set when they register, and shown on their marketplace profile:

| Term | Range | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Profit share** | 10% – 30% | 20% |
| **Leverage band** | Manager's choice | 10× – 20× |
| **Maximum followers** | Manager's choice | 100 |

**The profit share is the manager's stated share of profits generated for followers.** It is
declared at signup and displayed as part of the terms you are accepting when you follow them.

**The leverage band is the range the manager says they trade in.** Treat it as a description of the
strategy rather than a limit enforced on your position — the leverage *your* copied trade uses is
the one you set in your own copy settings, and it is not clamped to the manager's band.

:::warning[Read the band as risk, not as a promise]
A manager publishing a 10×–20× band is telling you the strategy is leveraged throughout. That is
a statement about the volatility of the returns you are signing up for. It is not a cap, and
nothing in your copy settings inherits it.
:::

## Following a fund manager

The flow is the marketplace flow, unchanged:

1. **Switch to the Fund Managers tab.** The marketplace has three: **All Providers**,
   **Signal Providers** and **Fund Managers**. They are copied on the same mechanics but charged on
   different terms, so it is worth knowing which you are looking at.
2. **Read the profile as you would any provider.** Return, maximum drawdown, win rate, trade count
   and track record length all mean the same thing here.
3. **Press Allocate.** The card's action button reads **Subscribe** on a signal provider and
   **Allocate** on a fund manager. It leads to the same copy settings either way.
4. **Set your copy mode.** Your sizing is yours. A fund manager's profit share does not change how
   big your copied position is.

On the marketplace card, the stat a signal provider shows as **Fee** a fund manager shows as
**Profit Share**, expressed as a percentage. That single field is the quickest way to tell the two
apart at a glance.

→ [Browse the Marketplace](/docs/social-trading/browse-marketplace)
→ [Subscribe to a Provider](/docs/social-trading/subscribe-provider)

## What is the same, and what is not

Worth being precise about, because the differences are narrower than the two names suggest.

**The same:**

- The three copy modes, and the 100% cap on Fixed Ratio
- Leverage and margin mode, set by you, per subscription
- The absence of a symbol filter and of an automatic loss circuit breaker — pausing is manual for
  both provider types
- The reasons your result diverges from the published one

**Different:**

- Where the manager's account came from, and who holds its keys
- How the manager is paid, and therefore what their incentive is
- The **Profit-Sharing** badge and the type filter in the marketplace

:::tip[The incentive is the thing to actually think about]
A subscription fee is earned whether you make money or not. A profit share is only earned when the
follower's side shows a profit — which aligns the manager with you on direction, and *misaligns*
them on risk, because the upside of a leveraged bet is shared and the downside is yours. Neither
model is safer than the other. They fail differently.
:::

The provider signup flow asks which of the two you are registering as before it asks anything else,
and the fund-manager branch is where the sub-account gets provisioned.
