# Concepts

**Guides tell you which buttons to press. These pages tell you what happens after you press
them.**

Read them when a guide worked but you want to know why, when something behaved differently from
what you expected, or before you automate anything with real money — automation removes the
moment where a human would have noticed something was wrong, and these pages are where the
replacement for that moment is described.

---

## Start here

**[The Account Model](/docs/concepts/account-model)** — The login account, trading slots, and the
Funding Wallet are three different things that everyday language calls "your account". Almost
every question about where money sits, or why a feature is greyed out, resolves here.

**[How a Signal Becomes a Trade](/docs/concepts/signal-pipeline)** — The path from a webhook POST
or a Telegram message to a fill, and every point at which it can be rejected instead. This is the
page to read when an alert fired and nothing happened.

---

## Execution

**[How Orders Execute](/docs/concepts/order-execution)** — Trigger and fill are different events.
That distinction explains why a stop loss can fill worse than its stop price, why a stop-limit can
fail to fill at all, and what a take-profit ladder actually changes about your outcome.

**[How Copy Trading Executes](/docs/concepts/copy-trading-mechanics)** — A provider's trade is
translated into your account's terms, not duplicated. Why your result structurally differs from
their published performance, and which multiplier reproduces their risk rather than their size.

---

## Money and safety

**[Funding and Settlement](/docs/concepts/funding-and-settlement)** — Where a deposit goes,
why everything settles in USDC, and why withdrawals need two codes from two channels.

**[Risk Controls](/docs/concepts/risk-controls)** — Every limit that can stop or modify a trade,
which layer it lives on, and a defensible starting configuration for a source you have not yet
verified. Worth reading before your first automated trade rather than after it.
