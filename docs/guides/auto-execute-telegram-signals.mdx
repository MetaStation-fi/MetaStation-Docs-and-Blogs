# Auto-Execute Telegram Signals

**The job:** a signal is posted in a Telegram channel, and the trade opens on your account
automatically — parsed from the message, sized by your rules, not the caller's.

**Time:** about 10 minutes. **You need:** a Telegram account, access to the signal channel, and a
slot with automation (free on your MetaStation Account).

:::caution[You are automating someone else's judgement]
This is the highest-trust thing you can wire into a trading account. A channel that occasionally
posts an oversized "high conviction" call will act on that conviction with your balance unless you
have capped it. Step 4 is not optional here — do it before mapping any channel.
:::

---

## Step 1 — Connect your Telegram account

Open **Automation** and switch to the **Telegram connection** tab
([metastation.fi/social-trade/automation?tab=telegram](https://metastation.fi/social-trade/automation?tab=telegram)).

1. Enter your **phone number with country code** — for example `+1 234 567 8900`.
2. Click **Connect Telegram**.
3. Telegram sends a code to your Telegram app. Enter it under **Verification Code**.
4. If your *Telegram* account has two-step verification enabled, you are asked for that password
   next. That is Telegram's password, not your MetaStation one.

{/* SCREENSHOT: guide-tg-connect | Telegram connection tab, phone number entry state | 1440x900 | redact: phone number */}

:::warning
Your Telegram session is stored encrypted and MetaStation does not read your private messages.
Never share a Telegram login code with anyone — MetaStation support will never ask for it.
:::

## Step 2 — Find the channel

Channels you have **joined with your connected Telegram account** are listed automatically — there is
nothing to forward. Joined the channel just now? Press **Refresh Channels**.

The connection card shows how many channels were detected and how many are set to copy trade or
broadcast.

## Step 3 — Map the channel to an account slot

1. Open the account card for the slot you want to trade on.
2. Press **Add Channel** and **Select Signal Channel**.
3. Save. The channel shows as **Listening**.

:::note[Three channels per slot]
Up to **3 signal channels per account slot**. If you follow more sources than that, map different
channels to different slots.
:::

## Step 4 — Cap what the channel can do

Two places, and both matter.

**On the channel** — edit it on the account card and set **Who can trigger trades**: anyone who
posts, **channel admins only**, bots only, or **pinned messages only**. Admins only keeps a stray
reply from being read as a call.

**On the slot** — open **Automation → Your Webhook URLs** and press **Trade Setting**. These caps
apply to every signal the slot executes, Telegram's included, and override what the call says:

| Control | Why it matters here |
|---|---|
| **Leverage** | A `50x` call executes at your cap instead. This is the setting that separates a loss from a liquidation |
| **Percentage of Balance** or **USD in Value** | The caller decides direction; you decide exposure. A call sized above it is cut down, and a call with no size uses it |
| **Use this as maximum risk setting** | Turns the caps on |

A defensible starting point for an unproven channel: leverage capped at 3x–5x and a size of 1–2% of
balance, on a slot of its own.

→ [Risk Controls](/docs/concepts/risk-controls)

## Step 5 — Watch it parse before you trust it

MetaStation reads ordinary signal formatting. A message like this parses cleanly:

```
📈 LONG BTC/USDT
Entry: Market
TP1: 48000
TP2: 51000
SL: 41000
Leverage: 10x
```

The parser extracts action, symbol, direction, entry, take-profit levels, stop loss and leverage.

**If a signal is ambiguous or missing something required, it logs a parse error and executes
nothing.** It never guesses — a wrong trade costs more than a missed one.

Let a channel run for a few real signals before you size up. Channels that post prose commentary or
images will produce parse errors, and it is better to learn that at 1% than at 10%.

An **edit** to a call before it is placed amends it; a **deletion** withdraws it. The same call
posted in two of your channels is placed once.

## Step 6 — Monitor and disconnect

**History → Signal History** shows every signal, its **Processing Status** and, if it did not
execute, the **Reason**. **Results by channel** on the account card shows each channel's results.
To stop a channel for a while, set it from **Listening** to paused.

**Disconnect** ends the session and signal parsing stops immediately. Reconnect any time by
repeating the phone-number flow.

## When a signal does not trade

| Symptom | Cause |
|---|---|
| Channel never appeared | Your connected Telegram account has not joined it. Join it in Telegram, then press **Refresh Channels** |
| No slot available to map | The target slot has no automation — exchange slots need a Store plan |
| A channel stopped trading | It was paused — by you, or because the exchange slot's plan expired |
| Message logged with a parse error | The format was not understood. Commentary and image-only posts do not parse |
| Listed but not executed | Its **Reason** says why — a late call, no size, or the exchange refused it |
| Traded smaller than the call | The slot's Trade Setting capped it, which is the intended behaviour |
