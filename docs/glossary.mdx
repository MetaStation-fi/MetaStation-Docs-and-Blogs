# Glossary

Terms used across these docs, defined once. Platform-specific terms are marked
**(MetaStation)**; the rest are general trading vocabulary as it applies here.

---

## Accounts and structure

#### Login account
Your identity on MetaStation — what you sign in with (e-mail and password, Google, or Telegram) plus
2FA. It owns your trading slots but is not itself a place where funds sit. → [Account Model](/docs/concepts/account-model)

#### Slot **(MetaStation)**
One trading configuration: a venue, its balance, its settings, its automation rules and its own
webhook URL. An account can hold several. Slots are independent — a signal sent to one cannot
affect another. → [Account Slots](/docs/trading/account-slots)

#### MetaStation Account **(MetaStation)**
The native trading slot; your first is created at registration. Web3 by default, trades on-chain in
USDC, and includes every automation tool free. Activated by its first deposit, which pays a one-time
activation fee.

#### Connected exchange slot **(MetaStation)**
A Binance, ByBit or KuCoin account linked with your own API keys. Funds stay at the exchange;
MetaStation places orders. Free for manual trading; automation needs a Store plan for the slot.

#### Funding Wallet **(MetaStation)**
The wallet on Arbitrum One where a MetaStation Account's deposits land, as USDC. Created for you;
no seed phrase. By hand it accepts USDC on Arbitrum One only. → [Funding and Settlement](/docs/concepts/funding-and-settlement)

#### Manual slot **(MetaStation)**
An exchange slot without an automation plan. Terminal trading and copy-trading as a follower work;
webhooks and Telegram-to-Trade do not.

#### Activation fee **(MetaStation)**
A one-time fee taken from a MetaStation Account's first deposit. The Deposit page shows it, and the
minimum first deposit, before you send.

#### Enable Trading **(MetaStation)**
A one-time step on a MetaStation Account before its first order.

## Markets and instruments

#### Spot
Buying or selling the asset itself. You own what you bought; there is no leverage and no
liquidation.

#### Perpetual futures (perps)
A futures contract with no expiry date. It tracks the underlying price and can be held
indefinitely, held to that price by funding payments. Where leverage on MetaStation is used.

#### Funding rate
Periodic payments between long and short holders of a perpetual that keep its price tethered to
spot. A cost of *holding* a position, independent of whether price moved your way. Settles in
USDC.

#### Leverage
Trading a position larger than your margin. `10x` means $1,000 of margin controls a $10,000
position — and moves your balance ten times as fast in both directions.

#### Liquidation
Forced closure when losses consume the margin backing a position. What leverage risks, and the
reason a leverage cap is the highest-value automation setting.

#### USDC
The dollar stablecoin MetaStation settles in. Margin, profit, loss and fees are all USDC.

## Prices

#### Mark price
A smoothed reference price, resistant to brief wicks and thin-book spikes. The **default** trigger
price for stops and SLX, because a single outlier trade should not close a position the wider
market never moved against.

#### Last price
The most recent actual trade. Reacts to every print, including outliers — triggering on it fires
stops more readily.

#### Index price
An aggregate across external venues. Least affected by any single venue's behaviour.

#### Slippage
The gap between the price that triggered an order and the price it actually filled at. The cost of
demanding certainty of execution in a fast market.

## Orders

#### Market order
Executes immediately at the best available price. Always fills; the price is not guaranteed.

#### Limit order
Executes at your price or better. The price is guaranteed; the fill is not.

#### Stop market
A market order triggered when price reaches your stop level. Always closes you — possibly at a
materially worse price than the stop. Available in the terminal; over the webhook API this is
configured through `futurestopLoss`, not as an `orderType`.

#### Stop limit
A limit order triggered at your stop level. Protects the price and **can fail to fill entirely**,
leaving the position open and the loss running.

#### Trigger vs. fill
Two separate events: the condition being met, and a trade actually happening. Price keeps moving
between them. → [Order Execution](/docs/concepts/order-execution)

#### Take profit (TP)
An order closing part or all of a position at a favourable price. Up to **10** per position on
MetaStation.

#### TP ladder
Several take-profit levels at rising prices, each against a slice of the position. Changes the
shape of the outcome rather than the edge.
→ [Scale Out with Multiple TPs](/docs/guides/scale-out-with-take-profits)

#### TP `amount` **(MetaStation)**
The quantity closed at one take-profit level over the webhook API, expressed as a number of base
tokens. Percentage strings and keywords are rejected, and the total across levels must not exceed
the order quantity. The trading terminal additionally accepts USD and percentage entry.

#### Stop loss (SL)
An order closing a position at a defined loss. A position without one has no defined worst case.

#### Trailing stop
A stop that ratchets up behind the peak price and never moves back down. Follows *price*.

#### SLX (Stop Loss X) **(MetaStation)**
An advanced stop system that follows *take-profit events* rather than price. Three modes:
`breakeven` (move the stop to entry once a chosen TP fills), `follow_tp` (move it to the previous
TP after each fill), and `callback_rate` (trail by a percentage from the peak).
→ [Risk-Free Trade with SLX](/docs/guides/risk-free-trade-with-slx)

#### Callback rate
In SLX, the percentage below the highest price seen since activation at which the stop sits.

## Margin

#### Isolated margin
Only the margin allocated to a position is at risk. A liquidation cannot take the rest of the
balance. The safer default for automation, because the worst case is knowable in advance.

#### Cross margin
The whole slot balance backs every position. More room before liquidation, and the entire account
standing behind each trade.

## Automation

#### Webhook
An HTTP endpoint that receives trading signals. Your webhook URL ends in a token unique to one
slot. → [Webhook Trading](/docs/automation/webhook-trading)

#### Webhook token **(MetaStation)**
The unique string at the end of a webhook URL. It is a **bearer credential** — the whole
credential — and it also determines which slot trades.
→ [Secure Your Webhook](/docs/guides/secure-your-webhook)

#### Regenerate Secret **(MetaStation)**
Issues a new webhook token and invalidates the old URL immediately. The response to any suspected
leak.

#### Payload
The JSON or natural-language body of a signal, describing the trade to execute.

#### Parse error **(MetaStation)**
Logged when a signal cannot be understood. **Nothing executes** — MetaStation never guesses at
missing values. → [Signal Pipeline](/docs/concepts/signal-pipeline)

#### Natural language format **(MetaStation)**
Human-readable signals — the kind Telegram channels post — accepted on the same endpoint as JSON.

#### Telegram-to-Trade **(MetaStation)**
Executing signals posted in a Telegram channel bound to one of your slots. Up to three channels
per slot.

#### Gasless **(MetaStation)**
Placing and cancelling orders on a MetaStation Account costs no network gas. You never hold a gas
token to trade. Moving money between wallets or withdrawing carries a small network fee in USDC.

## Social trading

#### Provider
Anyone listed in Social Trade whose trades followers copy into their own accounts: **strategies**,
**fund managers** and **signal providers**. MetaStation manages no funds.

#### Strategy
A tracked trading record that MetaStation scores. It is copied as its own single-strategy index once
it has finished its time tracked on paper and passes the checks.

#### Signal provider
A provider who publishes signals from a webhook or a Telegram channel. Paid a monthly price, prepaid
in the Store, or nothing.

#### Fund manager
A provider who trades **their own account** (connected exchange keys or a MetaStation Account) and
is paid a **performance fee** — a share of followers' profit, taken only when a copy exits in
profit. Copied through exactly the same path as a signal provider.
→ [Fund Managers](/docs/social-trading/fund-managers)

#### Index **(MetaStation)**
A set of scored strategies copied into your own account by fixed, published rules. MetaStation
places its orders; it does not manage your account. → [Indexes](/docs/social-trading/indexes)

#### Follower
A user whose account replicates a provider's trades.

#### Sizing mode
How each copy is sized, chosen at subscription and **fixed afterwards**: **a share of each trade**
(0.01%–100% of the provider's size), **a fixed amount per trade** (dollars of the coin) or **the same
size as the provider**. → [Copy Settings](/docs/social-trading/copy-settings)

#### Share of each trade
The sizing most followers want. Start from `(your balance / provider balance) × 100` — that
reproduces their *risk*, not their notional — then halve it while you learn how they trade. Capped
at 100%: you cannot copy larger than the provider.

#### Countertrade
Copying the opposite side of each of a provider's trades. Chosen at subscription and fixed; needs a
loss limit.

#### Loss limit
A copy subscription's own stop: after this many dollars lost on its copies, copying stops and the
open copies are closed, kept, or closed with the provider, as you chose.

#### Performance fee
A fund manager's (or an index's) percentage of followers' profit: 0% or a whole 2–99%, capped by the
manager's tier, taken only when a copy exits in profit and measured against its high-water mark.

#### Tier
A provider's level — New, Bronze, Silver or Gold — which caps their follower count and performance
fee. Re-checked weekly.

#### Score and grade
The 0–100 score every provider is ranked by, from six parts and never from raw return. Grades: S
from 85, A 75, B 65, C 55, D 40. → [How Providers Are Scored](/docs/social-trading/scoring)

#### Leverage range
The range of leverage a provider says their strategy runs at. It describes the strategy's
volatility; it does **not** set the leverage on your copies, which open at your own account's
leverage or one you set.

#### Drawdown
The peak-to-trough fall in an account or track record. The number a follower has to sit through,
and a better guide to whether a provider is followable than the headline return.

## Security

#### 2FA / TOTP
Two-factor authentication with an authenticator app. Once on, every sign-in asks for the code, and
every withdrawal is confirmed by it.

#### Withdrawal gating **(MetaStation)**
Every withdrawal is confirmed with one 6-digit code, tied to that withdrawal: your authenticator
code, or — without an authenticator — a code sent by e-mail, capped at $1,000 of withdrawals in 24
hours. → [Withdrawal Security](/docs/security/withdrawal-whitelist)

#### Webhook verification mode **(MetaStation)**
How a slot's webhook decides whether to accept a request: **URL only**, **Signed requests** (an HMAC
signature with your signing key), or **Source-address check** (only the charting alert service's
published addresses). The last two make a leaked URL unusable on its own.

#### Dead man's switch
An opt-in setting per account: if MetaStation loses contact with the exchange for 5 minutes, the
exchange cancels every resting order on the account. → [Dead Man's Switch](/docs/security/dead-mans-switch)

#### Exchange API key
Credentials generated at Binance, ByBit or KuCoin letting MetaStation place orders. Grant trading
permissions only — **never withdrawals**.
→ [API Key Management](/docs/security/api-key-management)
